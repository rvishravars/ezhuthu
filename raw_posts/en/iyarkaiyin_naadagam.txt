Hello friends!

After writing essays in quick succession in the beginning, I found myself taking a hiatus. It was one of my own making. Upon finishing my Master's degree, I had to devote all my time to searching for a job. Eventually, within a short span, I secured a position in the computing department at the very college where I had studied. During that period of prioritizing work, I attempted to continue writing, but it proved impossible. It was a stressful time; living abroad without a job is difficult.

We have all heard the familiar adage that life is a drama—from movies, lessons, and through our own lived experiences. It runs without pause like an unending serial play, continuing even while we sleep. We usually perceive its intensity ebbing and flowing. In this play, there is a distinct place for the spectator as well. Those who watch often behold an entire composed perspective that remains elusive to the actors themselves. Can this composed gaze become a vision—a darshan? Does nature have a role to play in the dramas we witness? Is it essential for us to perceive nature's participation? I attempt to answer these questions.

In my younger years, in Tamil Nadu, as soon as I woke up in the morning, I would walk to the inner courtyard of our home to see the continuation of the drama my father had directed the night before. What greeted me there was a profound stillness. Stillness is not merely remaining silent; in truth, there is no such thing as absolute silence. I perceive my mother brewing coffee through its rising aroma. From the whir of the fast-spinning fan, I know my father and sisters are still asleep. I watch the sun rise and listen to the cawing chorus of crows. Meanwhile, from the hiss of boiling water, I realize mother is pouring hot water from the vessel into the coffee filter.

I can anticipate what will happen next. Yes—in a couple of minutes, as the milk boils over, mother turns off the stove (a distinct click). Having offered the steaming milk before God, a minute later she pours it into the coffee tumbler. Carrying the first brew of the morning, she heads to father's room, wakes him, and encourages him to prepare for the day's play. If anyone ever tells you that coffee lacks nutrition, pay them no mind.

By spending an hour each morning in quietude—without speaking, without turning on devices like the television or radio—we can truly experience nature in its fullness. Here, guided solely by pure consciousness, we can perceive the vision of nature as a living drama. But can such mornings ever be found in the bustling rush of big cities?

One could argue that everything, including television, is part of nature. Yet devices like television and radio serve us a pre-packaged 'composition'. They leave us no work of piecing together subtle, fleeting occurrences. It is precisely this act of synthesis that allows us to experience the vision of nature.

Nature's blessings to my mother, who awakens each morning crawling into the lap of nature.
